What is a Pension Plan?

A pension plan (also called a retirement plan) is an investment product that helps you systematically build a retirement corpus during your working years, and then pays you a regular income (pension) after you retire.

India has no universal pension system for the private sector. If you don't create your own pension, you will have no income after retirement — unless you depend entirely on your children or deplete your savings.

A pension plan solves this: you invest regularly for 20–30 years, accumulate a large corpus, and then convert that into a guaranteed monthly income for the rest of your life.

Types of Pension Plans

Different plans suit different needs. Here's a clear breakdown.

🏛️

NPS (National Pension System)

Government-backed retirement scheme. Invest during working years, get 60% as lump sum at 60 (tax-free), use 40% to buy annuity (monthly pension). Low charges, market-linked returns.

  • Extra ₹50K tax deduction (80CCD1B)
  • Very low fund management charges
  • Equity + debt mix options
  • Government-backed security
📊

ULIP Pension Plan

Market-linked pension plan from insurance companies. Premium split between insurance cover and equity/debt investment. Returns are market-linked and subject to fund performance — not guaranteed. Good for those 15+ years from retirement. Past performance is not indicative of future returns.

  • Market-linked higher returns
  • Life cover + pension in one
  • Switch funds as needed
  • Tax benefits under 80C
🛡️

Traditional Pension / Annuity

Pay premium for 10–20 years, receive guaranteed pension for life from retirement age. No market risk, fully guaranteed. Ideal for those who want certainty over higher returns.

  • Guaranteed pension amount
  • No market risk at all
  • Joint-life option (spouse covered)
  • Return of purchase price option

How Much Corpus Do You Need?

The 25x rule: you need 25 times your annual retirement expenses as your corpus.

1

Estimate Monthly Expenses at Retirement

If you need ₹50,000/month today, add 7% inflation for years to retirement. Retiring in 20 years? You'll need ₹1.9L/month then.

2

Calculate Annual Retirement Income Needed

₹1.9L/month × 12 = ₹22.8L per year. You need a corpus that can generate this amount annually for 25–30 years.

3

Apply the 25x Rule

₹22.8L × 25 = ₹5.7 Crore required corpus at retirement. This sounds large — but if you start at 30, ₹15,000/month in NPS/ULIP can build ₹6+ crore by age 60.

4

Account for Other Income Sources

Subtract EPF/PPF corpus, rental income, other investments from the target. The remaining gap is what your pension plan needs to fill.

5

Start Today — Every Year Counts

Starting at 30 vs 40 for the same corpus: at 30, you need ₹10,000/month. At 40, you need ₹30,000+/month. The math heavily rewards early starters.
